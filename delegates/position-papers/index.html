Position Paper Guide

What a position paper is, how to structure one and when it is due.

On this page

  1. What a position paper is
  2. Suggested structure
  3. Format and submission
  4. Writing well

Draft. This page is a draft based on standard Model UN practice. The Secretariat will publish the official version before the conference.

Confirmed

  • Position papers are due November 1, 2026.
  • A submitted position paper is required to be considered for an award.

What a position paper is

A position paper is a short document that sets out your assigned country’s view on a committee topic and what it wants the committee to do. Writing it is the best preparation for debate: it forces you to research before you arrive.

Suggested structure

  1. Background. Summarise the issue and why it matters to the international community.
  2. Your country’s position. Explain how the issue affects your country and what it has said or done about it.
  3. Past international action. Note the relevant treaties, resolutions and programmes, and where they fell short.
  4. Proposed solutions. Set out the specific measures your country wants the committee to adopt.

Format and submission

Length, formatting and how to submit your paper will be confirmed by the Secretariat. Write one paper per topic and cite your sources.

Writing well

  • Write as your country, not as yourself: “The Republic of … believes”, never “I think”.
  • Use official sources first: UN documents, your government’s statements and reputable news.
  • Be specific. A named programme with a funding source is stronger than a call to “raise awareness”.
  • The work must be your own. Plagiarised papers will not be considered.
