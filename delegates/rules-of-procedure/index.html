Rules of Procedure

How debate runs in committee: the flow of a session, points, motions and voting.

Draft. This page is a draft based on standard Model UN practice. The Secretariat will publish the official version before the conference.

Scope

These rules apply to every committee at the conference unless the committee’s study guide says otherwise. The chairs interpret the rules in committee, and the Secretariat’s decision on any question of procedure is final.

The working language of the conference is English.

Flow of debate

  1. Roll call. Delegates answer “present” or “present and voting”. A delegate who is present and voting may not abstain on substantive votes.
  2. Setting the agenda. The committee votes on which topic to debate first.
  3. General speakers’ list. Delegates give opening speeches setting out their country’s position.
  4. Moderated caucus. A faster debate on one specific question, with short speeches and a set time limit.
  5. Unmoderated caucus. Delegates leave their seats to negotiate, form blocs and write together.
  6. Working papers. Blocs put their ideas on paper and present them to the committee.
  7. Draft resolutions. Working papers are refined into formal draft resolutions with sponsors and signatories.
  8. Amendments. Delegates propose changes to draft resolutions.
  9. Voting procedure. The committee votes on amendments and then on each draft resolution.

Points

PointUse it to
Point of orderFlag a mistake in procedure by the chair or another delegate.
Point of personal privilegeRaise a personal discomfort, such as not being able to hear the speaker.
Point of parliamentary inquiryAsk the chair a question about the rules or what happens next.
Point of informationAsk a question of a speaker who has agreed to take questions.

Common motions

Voting

Procedural votes decide how the committee runs, and every delegate must vote. Substantive votes decide on amendments and draft resolutions; delegates may vote in favour, against or abstain, unless they answered “present and voting” at roll call.

Crisis committees

Crisis committees use shorter documents called directives and respond to updates from the dais. The specific crisis procedure will be set out in the committee’s study guide.
